# Spatial Transcriptomics Quality Control (Sequencing-Based)

## Introduction: The Spatial QC Paradigm

In sequencing-based spatial transcriptomics (e.g., 10x Visium and Visium HD), primary upstream pipelines such as Space Ranger process raw sequencing reads (FASTQ) and high-resolution histology images into count matrices, spatial coordinates, and S4 object representations. 

Before proceeding to downstream normalization, dimensional reduction, spatial domain clustering, or cell-type deconvolution, we must perform rigorous **Quality Control (QC)**. Quality control serves as the critical gatekeeper that identifies and excludes technical noise, compromised tissue regions, and experimental artifacts.

### The Fundamental Split: Dissociated Single Cells vs. Spatial Grids

In single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), quality control evaluates individual, dissociated cells suspended in droplets or wells. In contrast, sequencing-based spatial transcriptomics introduces unique analytical complexities:

1. **Spatial Units as Mixed or Binned Observations:** Rather than analyzing isolated cells, measurements are anchored to physical capture units, either fixed 55 µm spots (classic Visium), multi-resolution binned grids (2 µm, 8 µm, or 16 µm squares in Visium HD), or post hoc segmented polygons.
2. **Spatial Coherence of Technical Artifacts:** In scRNA-seq, damaged or dying cells are randomly distributed across droplets. In spatial tissue sections, technical defects physically affect **contiguous, multi-spot spatial regions**. Common physical artifacts include:
   * **Tissue folds and tears:** Cause overlapping cellular layers or localized detachment.
   * **Air bubbles and drying edges:** Impair permeabilization or enzymatic reaction efficiency.
   * **Staining inconsistencies and hangnails:** Alter local optical and transcript capture properties.
3. **Severe Downstream Impact:** Because spatial algorithms exploit neighborhood structures, uncorrected spatial artifacts induce false transcriptional gradients. This creates artificial clusters, corrupts spatial domain boundaries, skews cell-type deconvolution, and produces false-positive Spatially Variable Genes (SVGs).

## Macro-Level Quality Gates: Space Ranger (`web_summary.html`)

Quality control follows a hierarchical progression. Before inspecting individual spots or loading data into computational environments like R or Python, we evaluate **macro-level run metrics** generated by Space Ranger in its HTML run report (`web_summary.html`). If macro-level sequencing or alignment parameters fail, spot-level filtering cannot rescue the dataset.

| Metric Category | Key Parameter | Standard Benchmark | Biological & Technical Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sequencing** | **Q30 Bases in RNA Read** | $>75\text{–}80\%$ | Base-calling accuracy across flowcell cycles. |
| | **Sequencing Saturation** | $50\text{–}80\%$ | Library sequencing depth relative to library complexity. |
| **Mapping** | **Reads Mapped to Genome** | $\sim 85\%$ | General alignment efficiency against reference genome. |
| | **Mapped Confidently to Transcriptome** | $\sim 70\text{–}75\%$ | Reads mapping unambiguously to exonic transcript regions. |
| | **Intronic / Intergenic Rates** | Intronic: $\sim 1\text{–}3\%$<br>Intergenic: $<5\text{–}10\%$ | Elevated rates signal genomic DNA contamination or pre-mRNA leakage. |
| **Spot / Bin** | **Spots Under Tissue** | Slide-dependent | Total number of capture units intersecting the tissue mask. |
| | **Mean Reads & UMIs / Bin** | e.g., $>800$ reads, $>200$ UMIs (8 µm) | Overall transcript capture sensitivity and molecular yield. |
| **Image QC** | **Fiducial & Tissue Alignment** | Visual Overlay Verification | Confirms precise spatial registration between coordinate arrays and the histology image. |

## Spot- and Bin-Level Quality Metrics

Once macro-level quality is established, we compute three fundamental metrics for every individual spot or bin across the tissue section:

1. **Library Size (`sum` / `nCounts`):**
   * The total sum of Unique Molecular Identifiers (UMIs) detected per spatial unit.
   * *Low values* reflect low cellular density, inefficient permeabilization, or acellular connective tissue.
   * *Abnormally high values* frequently highlight tissue folds, multi-layer overlaps, or optical artifacts.
2. **Transcriptional Diversity (`detected` / `nGenes`):**
   * The total number of distinct genes with non-zero expression in that spatial unit.
   * Tracks closely with library size and measures transcriptomic complexity.
3. **Mitochondrial Expression Fraction (`subsets_Mito_percent`):**
   * The percentage of total UMIs derived from the mitochondrial genome (genes prefixed with `MT-` or `mt-`).
   * Because mitochondrial membranes are more resilient than cytoplasmic membranes, damaged, dying, or lysed cells leak cytoplasmic transcripts while retaining mitochondria, resulting in high mitochondrial fractions.

## Global Outliers vs. Spatially-Aware Filtering (The Spatial QC Dilemma)

### The Failure of Global Thresholds in Spatial Omics

In standard scRNA-seq workflows, quality control applies **global thresholds** across the entire sample, typically filtering out observations exceeding 3 Median Absolute Deviations (MADs) from the sample median:

$$
\text{Threshold} = \text{Median} \pm 3 \times \text{MAD}
$$

where the Median Absolute Deviation ($\text{MAD}$) measures the statistical dispersion around the median:

$$
\text{MAD} = \text{median}\left(|x - \text{median}(x)|\right)
$$

In spatially-aware methods (such as `SpotSweeper`), this dispersion is evaluated locally ($\text{MAD}_i$) over the spot's $k$-nearest spatial neighborhood $\mathcal{N}_i$:

$$
\text{MAD}_i = \text{median}_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i}\left(|x_j - m_i|\right)
$$

where $m_i = \text{median}_{j \in \mathcal{N}_i}(x_j)$ represents the local neighborhood median.

* Tissues naturally contain distinct anatomical zones characterized by radically different cell densities, cell sizes, and metabolic activities (e.g., dense cortical neuronal layers vs. sparse white matter in the brain, or metabolically quiet tendon fibers vs. active skeletal muscle).
* Applying a single global threshold treats low-density or low-metabolism anatomical regions as technical failures, erroneously discarding healthy, valid biological tissue layers.

### Spatial Autocorrelation as Biological Signal

In spatial transcriptomics, library size is not purely technical noise. It exhibits high **spatial autocorrelation** (measured by a high Moran's $I$ index), reflecting genuine histological architecture:

$$
\text{Spatial Variation} = \text{Histological Density (Biological)} + \text{Technical Noise}
$$

To protect valid biological structures, quality control must evaluate spots relative to their **immediate physical spatial neighborhood** rather than against the global slide distribution.

## Spatially-Aware Quality Control: The SpotSweeper Framework

`SpotSweeper` is a specialized Bioconductor package developed to detect technical spatial artifacts in high-density spatial transcriptomics while rigorously preserving biological heterogeneity. It operates across two complementary spatial scales:

```text
===================================================================================
                          SPOTSWEEPER QC WORKFLOW
===================================================================================

  1. Spot-Level Local QC (Focal Anomalies)
  ├── Construct spatial k-NN graph based on physical (x,y) coordinates
  ├── Calculate local robust metrics within neighborhood: median (m_i) and MAD_i
  ├── Compute local Z-score:
  │     z_i = 0.6745 * (x_i - m_i) / MAD_i
  └── Flag localized outliers (single damaged cells or isolated spot dropouts)

  2. Region-Level Artifact QC (Contiguous Multi-Spot Defects)
  ├── Quantify local variance of mitochondrial ratio across multiple spatial radii
  ├── Regress out mean-variance dependency via linear modeling
  ├── Run Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on mean-corrected multiscale variances
  └── Apply k-means clustering (k = 2) on PC1 & PC2 to separate technical artifacts
      (e.g., drying edges, tissue folds, dissection hangnails) from intact tissue
===================================================================================
```

### Spot-Level Local Outliers
For focal, isolated defects, `SpotSweeper` constructs a $k$-nearest neighbor ($k$-NN) graph in physical $(x, y)$ coordinate space (typically $k = 18\text{–}36$ neighbors). For each spot $i$, it computes a standardized local $Z$-score relative to its local median ($m_i$) and local median absolute deviation ($\text{MAD}_i$):

$$
z_i = 0.6745 \times \frac{x_i - m_i}{\text{MAD}_i}
$$

Spots with $|z_i| > 3$ are flagged as local outliers. By comparing a spot only to its immediate histological neighbors, a low-count spot in white matter is preserved because its neighbors are equally low-count, whereas a damaged spot surrounded by healthy cortex is cleanly identified and removed. In practice, because purely local neighborhoods are blind to wide, contiguous tears (where all neighboring units share uniformly degraded metrics), local outlier detection is paired with a **relaxed global floor** (`global_outliers | local_outliers`).

### Multi-Spot Contiguous Artifact Detection
Large technical defects—such as incomplete reagent coverage ("dryspots") or dissection damage ("hangnails")—often exhibit artificially compressed local variance in mitochondrial proportions.

`SpotSweeper::findArtifacts()` attempts to detect these regions by:
1. Calculating the local variance of mitochondrial fractions across multiple neighborhood radii.
2. Correcting for the technical mean-variance relationship using linear regression.
3. Performing Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the corrected multiscale variance matrix.
4. Partitioning the tissue into two clusters ($k = 2$) via $k$-means clustering on PC1 and PC2, flagging the lower-variance cluster as a regional artifact.

> **Methodological Caveat:** Because `findArtifacts()` enforces a hardcoded binary split ($k = 2$) calibrated on homogeneous brain sections, it cannot report the *absence* of a regional artifact. In highly heterogeneous tissues (such as solid tumors), it can mistake genuine histopathological compartments with uniform metabolic profiles for technical damage, requiring strict visual verification on the tissue image before use.

## Practical Bioinformatic Implementation Workflow

In Bioconductor pipelines (`SpatialExperiment` or `SpatialFeatureExperiment`), spatial quality control follows four structured steps:

1. **Calculate per-unit QC metrics:**
   Using `scuttle::addPerCellQCMetrics()` (or C++-accelerated `scrapper::quickRnaQc()`), we populate `colData` with `sum`, `detected`, and `subsets_Mito_percent`.
2. **Diagnostic spatial visualization:**
   Using `Voyager::plotSpatialFeature()` (for `sfe` cell polygons) or `ggspavis::plotCoords()` / `plotVisium()` (for `spe` binned grids), we project QC metrics over physical tissue coordinates and H&E histology to inspect spatial gradients, edge tears, and localized mitochondrial spikes.
3. **Execute two-tiered filtering:**
   We combine a relaxed global technical floor with `SpotSweeper::localOutliers()` to flag compromised units while preserving low-density stroma and metabolically active structures (evaluating `findArtifacts()` critically against histology).
4. **Sequential 2D subsetting:**
   We first prune flagged observations (`!discard`), and subsequently remove unexpressed genes (`rowSums(counts) > 0`) to prevent zero-variance errors in downstream normalization (`scuttle::logNormCounts()`) and dimensionality reduction.
